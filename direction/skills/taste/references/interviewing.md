# Interviewing (guided mode)

Guided mode is **experimental**. Discovery quality has not been scored yet. Prefer open questions about the
business and the audience. Stop when the Context and Values section can be written honestly.

## Hard rules

1. **Ask one useful question at a time.** Wait for the answer before the next.
2. **No style labels as choices.** Do not ask the client to pick between "playful" and "calm", "minimal" and
   "bold", or any other look-words. Those labels produce the generic site for that label.
3. **Values belong to the client; the words for a look are open.** Ask what people worry about, what nearby
   options get wrong, what a good visit feels like in plain life — not which aesthetic name fits.
4. **Describe looks in plain qualities**, never style names the agent brought in. "The important information
   is much larger than the rest" is a quality. "Editorial" / "Swiss" / "brutalist" / "premium SaaS" are labels.
5. **No recommended answer** on questions about meaning, values or references. Recommendations return only
   when the conversation has reached a design decision (which direction, which constraint).
6. **Do not lead.** A leading question puts a label in the client's mouth. One accidental lead is recoverable;
   repeating a rejected label in new words is not.
7. **Infer and label.** Reasonable inferences are fine. Mark them `[inferred]`. Never upgrade them to
   `[client]` without the user saying so.
8. **Silence is not approval.** An inferred preference is not a chosen direction. Gates still need an explicit
   yes, a clear pick, or a written change request.

## What to learn before stopping discovery

Stop the interview when you can fill, without guessing silently:

- Audience (who decides, who uses)
- The one job of the page
- 2–5 values in the client's words (or clearly tagged `[inferred]` with confirmation pending)
- Positioning: the category, the alternatives the audience weighs, what those alternatives all do, the three
  things a visitor should take away in five seconds, and the price or care level (DIRECTION.md §3)
- What to avoid (observable)
- How to read any references they gave

Everything else can stay inferred or open.

## Question shapes that work

- "What's a visitor worried about when they first land?"
- "What do the other options nearby get wrong?"
- "Show me a site you like, and tell me which part."
- "What would make this site feel like it belongs to someone else?"
- "When someone leaves after one visit, what should they believe or do?"

## Positioning questions

Ask these after audience and the page's job are clear, one at a time. Two or three are usually enough; skip any
the brief already answers.

- "If someone didn't choose you, where would they go instead?" (alternatives)
- "What do all of those places do on their sites that customers rely on? And what do they all do that makes
  them blur together?" (conventions: the first half is Adopt, the second is Challenge or Avoid)
- "Someone looks at your page for five seconds and closes it. What three things should they be able to tell a
  friend about you?" (intended perception)
- "What do people pay you, compared with those alternatives, and what are they paying for?" (sophistication;
  never offer "premium" or "budget" as choices)
- "What's true about you that none of them could say?" (differentiate on)

Record the answers as `[client]`. Marking conventions Adopt, Challenge or Avoid stays `[agent]` until the user
confirms it at Gate 1.

## Question shapes that fail

- "Is the business playful and energetic, or calm and nurturing?"
- "Do you want a minimal premium look or something warmer?"
- "Would you say the brand is more Swiss or more editorial?"
- "Are you more premium or more affordable than the others?"
- "Should the site look like your competitors or stand out?" (asks for a verdict instead of facts)
- Any multiple-choice of style labels

## When the user already has a brief

If they paste a written brief, switch to **fast mode**: skip the interview, write Context and Values from the
brief, tag gaps as `[inferred]` or Open decisions, and go to directions. Do not re-interview for sport.

## Confirming Context and Values

Before directions, show the Context and Values section (with tags) and ask the user to confirm or correct it.
That confirmation is Gate 1. Do not treat "looks fine" about a later mock as confirmation of values.
